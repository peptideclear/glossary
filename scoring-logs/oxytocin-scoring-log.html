Scoring scope: intranasal administration for neuromodulatory effects
This RQS evaluates oxytocin specifically as used in the peptide/biohacking community — intranasal delivery for social cognition, anxiety, bonding, and neuromodulatory effects. The obstetric/labor induction evidence base (IV/IM administration) is extremely strong and would score in the Strong Evidence band independently. Combining the two evidence bases would overstate the evidence for the use case relevant to this catalog's audience.

Study Design
Multiple human RCTs evaluating intranasal oxytocin (IN-OT) for social cognition, anxiety, autism spectrum disorder, PTSD, and postpartum depression. Study design quality is genuinely RCT-level — randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trials are the norm in this literature. However, results across trials are inconsistent and several large RCTs (including ASD trials) have failed to show significant effects on primary endpoints. Scored as single RCT tier given the replication inconsistency dampening the multiple-RCT score.

Sample Size
Individual IN-OT trials typically enroll 20–80 participants. The 2017 meta-analysis (Keech et al.) synthesized 17 RCTs with 466 total NDD participants — moderate cumulative sample. The ASD trial literature is the most developed; some trials enroll 100+ but most remain in the 30–60 range. Meaningful but not large by therapeutic standard.

Replication
Multiple independent research groups globally (Netherlands, Germany, US, Australia) have conducted IN-OT trials. Replication exists but critically, findings have not consistently replicated across groups — different studies on the same endpoints (emotion recognition, social cognition, anxiety) produce conflicting results. This is a documented methodological challenge in the IN-OT literature, partly attributed to delivery variability and dosing inconsistency. Scored to reflect quantity of replication attempts, with deduction for inconsistency of results.

Note: Oxytocin's RQS score requires careful framing. The compound is one of the most studied neuropeptides in human history, with an impeccable clinical track record in obstetrics and lactation (IV/IM formulations, FDA approved). The IN-OT literature targeting social and cognitive effects is genuinely active and uses rigorous RCT methodology — but the results are mixed. A 2017 meta-analysis found positive but small effect sizes across neurodevelopmental disorders; a large 2021 ASD trial found no benefit on primary endpoints. The gap between the mechanistic plausibility (compelling) and the reproducible clinical signal (inconsistent) is the defining tension in this literature, and the score reflects that honestly.

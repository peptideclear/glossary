The RQS Reference

Every compound in the catalog, graded on the Research Quality Score: a 100-point measure of how strong the clinical research behind a compound actually is.

What this measures, and what it does not. The RQS grades the quality of the research evidence only. It is not a rating of how safe a compound is, how well it works, or whether you should use it. A low score means the science is thin or early, not that a compound is dangerous or useless. A high score means the research is rigorous, not that the compound is right for you. Those are different questions, and this sheet answers only the first.

The five bands

Strong 80–100 Moderate 60–79 Limited 40–59 Weak 20–39 Insufficient 0–19
Each score is built from seven weighted dimensions: Study Design (25), Sample Size (20), Replication (20), Journal Impact Factor (15), Funding Independence (10), Population Diversity (5), and Researcher h-Index (5). Full scoring logs for every compound live at peptideclear.com.

Scored by the PeptideClear editorial team, based on publicly available literature. The RQS reflects research quality only, never a safety or efficacy verdict.

Scores reflect the evidence as presented. Some compounds are scored on a specific approved indication or on a studied analog rather than the marketed form; see the individual scoring log for scoping notes. Compounds published in lower-impact-factor research ecosystems (for example, several Russian nootropic peptides) carry lower Journal Impact Factor scores that reflect the publishing environment, not necessarily the scientific quality.
